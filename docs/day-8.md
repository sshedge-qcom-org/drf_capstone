# Day 8 — Leadership discussion

*Sounding strategic, calm, and credible in leadership discussions*

<p class="fe-meta">Standard C1 · Lite · 2026-08-27</p>

## Professional Idioms { .fe-sec--idiom }

### 1. See the bigger picture

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--idiom">Idiom</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Understand the broader business, technical, or organizational context — not just the immediate task.
- **IPA:** `/siː ðə ˈbɪɡər ˈpɪktʃər/`
- **Formality:** Neutral

!!! failure "Avoid: We should think from high level."

!!! success "Say instead: We should see the bigger picture before deciding."

**Examples**

=== "Engineering"
    Before optimizing this one component, we need to see the bigger picture: the real bottleneck may be cross-service coordination.

=== "Meeting"
    I agree the implementation cost is high, but if we see the bigger picture, this reduces operational load across three teams.

=== "Presentation"
    To see the bigger picture, we looked beyond feature delivery and included supportability, ownership, and customer escalation trends.

### 2. Pick your battles

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--idiom">Idiom</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Decide which issues are worth challenging and which ones to let go.
- **IPA:** `/pɪk jʊr ˈbætəlz/`
- **Formality:** Neutral; slightly conversational

!!! failure "Avoid: We should not fight for each small point."

!!! success "Say instead: We need to pick our battles."

**Examples**

=== "Engineering"
    I don’t love the naming, but I’d rather pick our battles and focus on the unsafe concurrency pattern.

=== "Meeting"
    There are several things we could challenge, but let’s pick our battles and focus on the release risk.

=== "Presentation"
    The team had to pick its battles: we prioritized API stability over internal cleanup for this release.

### 3. Have a seat at the table

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--idiom">Idiom</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Be included in important discussions where decisions are made.
- **IPA:** `/hæv ə siːt æt ðə ˈteɪbəl/`
- **Formality:** Neutral

!!! failure "Avoid: Engineering should be present in decision meetings."

!!! success "Say instead: Engineering needs a seat at the table."

**Examples**

=== "Engineering"
    If platform decisions affect our roadmap, our team should have a seat at the table during architecture planning.

=== "Meeting"
    We don’t need to own the whole decision, but we do need a seat at the table before commitments are made.

=== "Presentation"
    One improvement this quarter was giving reliability engineering a seat at the table during product planning.

## Phrasal Verbs { .fe-sec--phrasal_verb }

### 1. Weigh in

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--phrasal_verb">Phrasal verb</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Give your opinion, especially when your expertise matters.
- **IPA:** `/weɪ ɪn/`

!!! failure "Avoid: Can architecture team give their inputs?"

!!! success "Say instead: Can the architecture team weigh in?"

**Examples**

=== "Technical-discussion"
    We should ask the security team to weigh in before we finalize the data-retention design.

=== "Code-review"
    This change affects the scheduler contract. Can someone from the platform team weigh in?

=== "Leadership"
    I’d like to weigh in on the delivery plan because the current sequence creates avoidable integration risk.

### 2. Step back

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--phrasal_verb">Phrasal verb</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Pause from details and look at the situation more broadly.
- **IPA:** `/stɛp bæk/`

!!! failure "Avoid: Let us come out of details and think broadly."

!!! success "Say instead: Let’s step back for a moment."

**Examples**

=== "Technical-discussion"
    Before debating the database schema, let’s step back and clarify the ownership model.

=== "Code-review"
    If we step back, this patch is mixing bug fix, refactor, and behavior change in one review.

=== "Leadership"
    Let’s step back and ask whether this roadmap still matches the customer priority.

### 3. Bring up

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--phrasal_verb">Phrasal verb</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Mention or introduce a topic for discussion.
- **IPA:** `/brɪŋ ʌp/`

!!! failure "Avoid: I want to discuss about one concern."

!!! success "Say instead: I want to bring up one concern."

**Examples**

=== "Technical-discussion"
    I want to bring up the dependency on the legacy service before we finalize the design.

=== "Code-review"
    Thanks for bringing up the test gap — I missed that edge case.

=== "Leadership"
    I’d like to bring up staffing risk now rather than wait until the release is already at risk.

## C1/C2 Vocabulary { .fe-sec--vocabulary }

### 1. Alignment

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--vocabulary">Vocabulary</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Shared understanding and agreement on direction, priorities, or decisions.
- **IPA:** `/əˈlaɪnmənt/`
- **Synonyms:** agreement, shared direction, consensus
- **Antonyms:** misalignment, disagreement, disconnect
- **Collocations:** strategic alignment, cross-team alignment, alignment on priorities, gain alignment

!!! failure "Avoid: We need to take alignment from all teams."

!!! success "Say instead: We need to get alignment across the teams."

**Examples**

=== "Software-engineering"
    We need cross-team alignment on API ownership before we commit to the migration timeline.

=== "Stakeholder"
    Before we publish the roadmap, I want to make sure we have alignment on scope, timing, and risk.

### 2. Accountability

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--vocabulary">Vocabulary</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Clear ownership and responsibility for outcomes, not just tasks.
- **IPA:** `/əˌkaʊntəˈbɪləti/`
- **Synonyms:** ownership, responsibility, answerability
- **Antonyms:** ambiguity, avoidance, lack of ownership
- **Collocations:** clear accountability, shared accountability, accountability model, accountability gap

!!! failure "Avoid: Who is responsible person for this?"

!!! success "Say instead: Who has accountability for this area?"

**Examples**

=== "Software-engineering"
    The design needs a clearer accountability model for incident response and service ownership.

=== "Stakeholder"
    We can deliver this safely if there’s clear accountability across development, validation, and release management.

### 3. Pragmatic

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--vocabulary">Vocabulary</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Practical and realistic, especially under constraints.
- **IPA:** `/præɡˈmætɪk/`
- **Synonyms:** practical, realistic, workable
- **Antonyms:** idealistic, theoretical, impractical
- **Collocations:** pragmatic approach, pragmatic decision, pragmatic compromise, pragmatic path forward

!!! failure "Avoid: This is practically solution."

!!! success "Say instead: This is a pragmatic solution."

**Examples**

=== "Software-engineering"
    A full rewrite would be cleaner, but a staged migration is the more pragmatic option.

=== "Stakeholder"
    Given the release timeline, we’re proposing a pragmatic path that reduces risk without delaying the milestone.

### 4. Escalation

<span class="fe-badges"><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--vocabulary">Vocabulary</span><span class="fe-badge fe-badge--new">New</span></span>

- **Meaning:** Raising an issue to higher-level owners or leadership because it needs attention, decision-making, or support.
- **IPA:** `/ˌɛskəˈleɪʃən/`
- **Synonyms:** elevation, escalation path, leadership attention
- **Antonyms:** local resolution, containment, closure
- **Collocations:** customer escalation, escalation path, escalation point, avoid escalation, handle an escalation

!!! failure "Avoid: This issue got escalated to upper people."

!!! success "Say instead: This became a leadership escalation."

**Examples**

=== "Software-engineering"
    We need a clear escalation path if the dependency team cannot meet the integration date.

=== "Stakeholder"
    This is not a customer escalation yet, but we should treat it as a risk that needs leadership visibility.

## Natural English Upgrade

| Common Indian corporate English | Natural international English | Why it's better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| We need to take alignment from leadership. | We need to get leadership alignment. | More idiomatic; “take alignment” sounds non-native. |
| Please give your inputs on this. | Could you weigh in on this? | Sounds more natural and senior in technical discussions. |
| Let us discuss about the concern. | Let’s discuss the concern. / I’d like to bring up a concern. | “Discuss about” is incorrect; “bring up” sounds natural. |
| Who is the responsible person? | Who owns this? / Who has accountability for this? | More concise and executive-friendly. |
| We should think in high level. | Let’s step back and look at the bigger picture. | Natural leadership framing. |
| This is practically possible. | This is a pragmatic option. | More polished and precise. |
| We need to avoid future escalations. | We need to reduce the risk of future escalations. | More natural and less absolute. |
| I have one point to tell. | I’d like to make one point. | Standard native phrasing. |
| We should not fight for small things. | We need to pick our battles. | More idiomatic and leadership-oriented. |
| Engineering should be involved in decision. | Engineering needs a seat at the table. | Stronger, more idiomatic leadership language. |

## Speaking Practice

!!! quote "Practice out loud"
    Answer these ALOUD. Keep each answer to 30–45 seconds. Focus on sounding calm, direct, and senior.

=== "Meeting"

    > Your team is being asked to commit to an aggressive roadmap. What would you say?

    :material-target: **Use:** `step back` · `pragmatic`

    > A stakeholder wants a decision before engineering has reviewed the risk. How do you respond?

    :material-target: **Use:** `weigh in` · `alignment`

    > Two teams disagree on ownership. How do you frame the issue?

    :material-target: **Use:** `accountability` · `bigger picture`

=== "Presentation"

    > Present a recommendation to leadership about reducing release risk.

    :material-target: **Use:** `pragmatic` · `escalation`

    > Explain why engineering should be involved earlier in roadmap decisions.

    :material-target: **Use:** `a seat at the table` · `alignment`

    > Summarize a difficult technical decision where you chose the practical path.

    :material-target: **Use:** `pick your battles` · `pragmatic`

=== "Leadership-discussion"

    > A senior leader asks why your team is pushing back on a date. What do you say?

    :material-target: **Use:** `step back` · `bigger picture`

    > You need to raise a dependency risk without sounding negative. How do you phrase it?

    :material-target: **Use:** `bring up` · `escalation`

    > You want another team’s expertise before finalizing a design. What do you say?

    :material-target: **Use:** `weigh in` · `accountability`
